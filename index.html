Model assumptions

Rectangular room; frequency-independent absorption. Maps use 2D image sources and spherical distance loss. The interference map assumes a single coherent steady tone; broadband noise will not produce the same pattern. Furniture, diffraction, floor/ceiling interference, microphone directivity and air absorption are not modeled.

RT60 includes all six room surfaces. It is a separate statistical estimate. Min/max markers include boundary samples; tied extrema use one representative location. Color limits only affect visualization. Export retains every computed cell value.

3D early-reflection model settings

Uses the selected phone's plan coordinates and orange source S. Includes floor and ceiling. No random rays. Finite early-response model; late reverberation is not synthesized.

Probe metrics: relative to the first detectable arrival (automatic onset, not guaranteed direct sound). Uncorrected, finite-window energy decay from the matched-filter response; no noise-floor subtraction or tail trimming. EDT (0 to −10 dB), T20 (−5 to −25 dB) and T30 (−5 to −35 dB) are raw regressions with fit quality; poor fits are flagged, not removed. Band-limited, not octave-band ISO processing.

Connect phones

Open this same page on every device. One device is the main screen; the others are listening phones. They find each other with a 4-letter room code.

On the main screen

  1. Press Create room.
  2. A 4-letter code appears at the top of the page.
  3. Read it out to everyone with a phone.

On each listening phone

  1. Give the phone a name you'll recognise.
  2. Type the room code and press Join room.
  3. Press Turn on this device's microphone at the top of the page.

Needs internet to find the other phones. The room code is an address, not a password: share it only with your group.

Connection details

The PeerJS library is embedded in this file. It contacts the public PeerJS signaling service and Google STUN; direct WebRTC carries measurements. No TURN relay is configured, so some guest, institutional, or mobile networks may fail. For phone microphone access, serve this HTML from a normal HTTPS static host. Opening a downloaded file on a phone may restrict microphone access.

A disconnected phone retries while its page stays open. The host remembers its position for the session. Reloading the host closes the room; export its layout first.

How it works

  1. Explore the example. The page opens with pretend data so you can try every graph before measuring anything.
  2. Describe your room in step 1: its size and whether it's hard and bare or soft and furnished.
  3. Connect phones. On a laptop or tablet press Connect phones → Create room. On each phone, open this same page, join with the code and turn on the microphone. The page must be served over HTTPS for microphones to work.
  4. Place the phones around the room at the same height, then drag their dots on the map to match.
  5. Read the map in step 2 and tap any phone to see what it hears in step 3.
  6. Run the echo test in step 4. One phone chirps, the rest listen, and you get the room's echo time and clarity.

Words you'll see

dB (decibel)
A loudness scale. +10 dB sounds about twice as loud; 3 dB is a small but noticeable change.
Echo time (RT60)
How long a sound takes to fade by 60 dB, roughly from a shout to silence.
Clarity (C50)
Whether speech stays crisp. Above 0 dB, most sound arrives early enough to help understanding.
Pitch mix (spectrum)
How much bass, middle and treble is in the sound right now.
Flutter echo
A fast "zing" of repeats between two parallel hard walls.
Technical notes from the original proof of concept
  1. Explore Demo to see the visualizations. Demo readings are synthetic.
  2. Use this independent HTML on a normal HTTPS host for real phone tests. Internet is needed to find the room.
  3. On the dashboard, choose Connect phones → Create room. On each phone, enter that four-character code.
  4. Tap Enable this phone on every sensor. This grants microphone access and unlocks test playback. Place phones in the room, then drag their markers on the host or type coordinates.
  5. Compare the live heatmap with the reflected-energy and interference models. Tune the frequency to explore stationary peaks and nulls. These simulated hot regions are not measurements.
  6. Select a sensor to see waveform, spectrum, spectrogram, session Leq, sample peak, crest factor, frequency centroid and clipping. Different phones need individual calibration before comparing physical sound levels.
  7. Run one room probe or probe each phone in turn. The selected phone emits a short chirp and all ready receivers analyze it. Click a results row to inspect its response.
  8. Export room geometry, phone positions, readings and probe results to JSON. Import restores geometry and matching phone positions; historical readings are not treated as live.

What the POC measures

Digital RMS and sample peak (dBFS), session energy mean from sampled windows, min/max, rolling L10/L50/L90 (up to 60 seconds), crest factor, clipping, FFT magnitude, dominant frequency, spectral centroid and octave-band FFT power. Baseline spikes and comfort alerts are configurable, not hearing-safety limits.

What remains approximate

Heatmap interpolation cannot reveal detail that no microphone sampled. Interference uses an ideal steady tone and room geometry, not synchronized phase measurements. The active probe uses a band-limited matched-filter response, not a calibrated laboratory impulse response. Echo delays are relative to the first automatically detectable arrival, not distances between phones. This onset can miss a very weak direct sound. Flutter is a heuristic for repeated peak spacing, not the Dietsch–Kraak perceptual criterion. EDT, T20, T30, Ts, C50, C80 and D50 are exploratory, finite-window estimates. No octave-band clarity claim is made: window-before-filtering would be required for that extension. Source and receiver directivity and phone response remain uncorrected. Phone gain control, speaker coloration, direct sound leakage and background noise can invalidate them.

All raw microphone audio stays on the receiving phone until deleted or the page is closed/reloaded. Only derived measurements, waveform previews, spectra and test curves are exchanged. Stop audio and Disconnect network end participation.

Research applied in this revision

  • Savioja & Svensson (2015): image-source geometry and the limits of geometrical modeling.
  • Vorländer (2013): material uncertainty matters. The ±α range here is a sensitivity scenario, not a statistical confidence interval.
  • ODEON / ISRA (2013): onset, truncation and window-before-filtering for octave-band clarity. This POC keeps probe metrics band-limited and unfiltered by octave.
  • ODEON flutter note: flutter and single echoes need separate interpretation. Our regular-spacing flag is only a candidate.
  • Pyroomacoustics: an architectural reference for early image sources and later ray tracing. No Python runtime is used in this HTML.

Raw-data policy: no FFT time averaging; no capture rejection for clipping or noise; no noise subtraction in response energy; no interior-only hotspot selection; full-grid and PCM exports. In the notes, noise compensation is a separate derived-analysis step: it is intentionally not applied to these raw displays. The new 3D response and the horizontal heatmaps are distinct models. The heatmaps still use the simpler 2D approximation. FDTD, diffraction, scattering and ISO certification are not implemented. Axial modes are listed analytically as guidance, not simulated wave amplitudes.
